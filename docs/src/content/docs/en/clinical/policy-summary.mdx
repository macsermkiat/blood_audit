## Bypass and review paths

The plain Hb tier is not the first check. Two order-level routing exits
run before any Hb reasoning (both default-ON):

- **All units returned un-transfused** (per the BDVSTTRANS unit ledger)
  → `RETURNED_NOT_TRANSFUSED`. No transfusion occurred; the order is
  excluded from appropriateness scoring (it remains visible as
  crossmatch-to-transfusion waste).
- **Declared surgical use / type-and-screen** (`BDVSTDT.USETYPE` code
  `2` → `surgery` or `3` → `type_screen`) → `PERIOP_TRANSFUSION_EXEMPT`.
  The clinician's declared use is authoritative; peri-op transfusions are
  not judged against the ward Hb policy. Also excluded from scoring.

After excluding missing Hb, the algorithm applies the global low-Hb rule
and then looks for other high-precedence evidence:

- **Hb < 7.0 g/dL** → deterministic `APPROPRIATE` before cohort routing.
- **MTP clinical context** → deterministic `APPROPRIATE`.
- **Delta-Hb trigger** → deterministic `APPROPRIATE` when the Hb drop meets:
  - ≥ 1.5 g/dL drop within 6 hours
  - ≥ 2.0 g/dL drop within 12 hours
  - ≥ 2.5 g/dL drop within 24 hours
- **Hemodilution or isolated low Hb without trend** → `NEEDS_REVIEW` only
  after the Hb < 7.0 rule has already had first chance to classify.
- **Unknown or non-threshold clinical context** → `NEEDS_REVIEW` when
  Hb is ≥ 7.0.

Legacy mode only (when the declared-use flag is forced off): a
peri-procedural anchor ≤ 6h before the order auto-clears, and a pre-op
crossmatch ≤ 72h before an upcoming operation **defers to the LLM**
rather than auto-clearing — a reservation is not an indication.

Vital-sign abnormalities alone (low BP, tachycardia) do not trigger the
bypass.

## Pre-op reservation screening (MSBOS)

Separately from the transfusion verdict, orders reserved for a planned
operation are screened against the MSBOS / OPBloodLimit reference
(default-ON). Reserving more units than the recommendation for the
planned procedure surfaces as `PREOP_OVER_RESERVATION`; the platelet leg
compares the pre-op platelet count against clinician-signed cutoffs
(MNS 80, neuraxial 100, cardiac-CPB 100, ×10³/µL). The screen never
guesses: unresolved procedures, ambiguous codes, and missing counts route
to review instead of auto-flagging.

## Exclusions and insufficient evidence

- Pediatric patients (age < 15) — different policy applies.
- Products other than the RBC allow-list (LPRC, LDPRC, SDR and the irradiated LDPRCI, LPRCI).
- ICD-10 conditions that take the patient out of scope per PR 17.2.

Orders without a documented pre-transfusion Hb are **not** classified as
appropriate or inappropriate. They are retained as `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`
so the QI committee can see the documentation gap.

## The 24 h minimum Hb

Where the rule above refers to a sub-threshold Hb, it means the LOWEST Hb in
the 24 hours before the order, counted from the most recent transfusion in
that window onward, not the value closest to the order time. A low value a
transfusion already corrected does not count. An Hb exactly at the cohort
floor is AT the floor, not below it.

## Platelet policy (Chula DRAFT, AABB/ICTMG 2025)

Platelet orders are judged against a separate policy with eight positive
indications and nine exclusion populations. As with the RBC rule, in the LLM
review an ICD-10 code alone never grounds an indication or an exclusion: the
condition must be documented in a note, a lab result, or an imaging report.
The deterministic prophylaxis auto-clear is the one exception by design: it
reads diagnosis codes only, under the hematology sign-off of 2026-09-20.

**Positive indications** (transfusion is appropriate only when the count is
below the stated threshold AND the indication is grounded in the notes):

1. Lumbar puncture: count < 50,000/µL (medicine or obstetrics) or
   < 80,000/µL (surgery or orthopaedics).
2. CVC insertion, thoracocentesis, arthrocentesis, or dental extraction:
   count < 50,000/µL. Bone marrow aspiration or biopsy grounds nothing at any
   count. An invasive procedure not on this list routes to `NEEDS_REVIEW`
   rather than a threshold of the reviewer's choosing.
3. Major non-neuraxial surgery, planned or performed within the previous
   24 h: count < 80,000/µL, or < 100,000/µL for a high-bleeding-risk patient
   or procedure.
4. Consumptive thrombocytopenia / DIC without active bleeding: count
   < 10,000/µL.
5. Chemotherapy or HSCT without active bleeding: count < 10,000/µL, or
   projected to fall below 10,000/µL within 24 h (a straight-line
   projection through the last two pre-order counts, computed by code, not
   a standing order's own trigger level).
6. Intracranial bleed (SDH, EDH, SAH, intracerebral): count < 100,000/µL
   while acute, acute-on-chronic, growing, or with neurosurgery planned;
   count < 50,000/µL when stable and non-operative, including a stable
   chronic SDH. An acute or acute-on-chronic bleed **stays acute for 7
   days** from the note or imaging that documents it; after 7 days the
   stable rule applies unless new imaging or a documented change shows
   growth or a new bleed.
7. Clinically significant active bleeding: count < 50,000/µL.
8. Aplastic anaemia on active therapy aimed at reversing the
   thrombocytopenia (ATG, cyclosporine, eltrombopag, or transplant
   work-up): count < 10,000/µL, or projected to fall below that within
   24 h; < 20,000/µL during the ATG course or with sepsis.

**Exclusion populations** (never appropriate to clear at a low count without
a separately-grounded positive indication): cardiac surgery / cardiopulmonary
bypass with no thrombocytopenia or severe bleeding; a head/intracranial
bleed with count > 100,000/µL; dengue fever without active bleeding; aplastic
anaemia that is chronic, stable, or not under active therapy, without active
bleeding (a patient on active therapy is indication 8, not this line);
snakebite without a life-threatening bleed; TTP; HIT; ITP; and chronic marrow
failure (e.g. myelodysplastic syndrome) not otherwise listed. For TTP, HIT,
and ITP, platelet transfusion can be actively harmful, so only the condition
named on that population's own line clears it; a procedure indication never
does.
